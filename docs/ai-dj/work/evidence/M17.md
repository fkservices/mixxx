All twenty changes had matching independently received host feedback and matching native fader pixels. Sixty screenshots were captured; their RGB fader classifier detected every frame at a valid endpoint. The helper sent exactly twenty changes, restored 127/127 and exited successfully. Nineteen changes were visibly confirmed within conservative 150 ms upper bounds. One change (zero-based trial 9) was only bounded to **191.96 ms**, so the full twenty-change target is **unresolved**. This is not evidence that Mixxx itself took 192 ms or missed the target: the screenshot interval spans an unknown exposure time. M19-UI-CAPTURE records the required finer-measurement follow-up.

## Calibration and uncertainty

The first warm-up screenshot took 462.79 ms; later warm-up captures took 82.72–93.45 ms. The sixty measurement calls ranged from 69.18 to 155.42 ms. This is variable-rate snapshot capture, not a fixed-rate video or a physical scanout measurement.

Ten file-handshake probes before and ten after capture relate the CUA monotonic performance clock to the helper's monotonic hrtime clock. The before offset intersection is 23,647,478.116–23,647,479.374 ms; after is 23,647,478.199–23,647,478.843 ms. The applied envelope spans both, plus 1 ms on each side: 23,647,477.116–23,647,480.374 ms. No wall clock is used. A fixed clock relationship over this short run is assumed and checked for consistency; unobserved nonlinear clock drift is not claimed measured.

Visible-by upper bounds use the end of the first screenshot call showing the requested fader position, conservatively translated to the helper clock, minus actual MIDI dispatch. Available old-state frames supply lower bounds from their call start. Screenshot exposure timestamps are unavailable; the method assumes fresh content captured within each invocation. Bounds range from 70.18 to 191.96 ms. They must not be summarized as exact repaint percentiles.
